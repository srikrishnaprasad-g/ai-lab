# ADR-008: Future-Proofing & Vendor Independence

## Purpose

Define the architectural principles that ensure AI-Lab remains adaptable as technologies, AI models, frameworks, and platforms evolve.

---

## Context

The AI ecosystem changes rapidly.

Frameworks, AI models, hosting providers, and development tools frequently evolve or become obsolete.

AI-Lab therefore adopts engineering principles that prioritize adaptability and minimize vendor lock-in.

---

## Decision

AI-Lab adopts a replaceable architecture where major technologies and platforms can be substituted with minimal impact on the overall engineering methodology.

The engineering process should remain stable even as implementation tools change.

---

## Future-Proofing Principles

### Modular Tooling

Each tool should have a clearly defined responsibility.

Replacing one tool should not require redesigning the overall workflow.

---

### Vendor Independence

Avoid unnecessary coupling to proprietary services whenever practical.

Use open standards and portable formats whenever feasible.

---

### AI Model Independence

Engineering processes should not depend on a specific LLM or AI provider.

The workflow should support replacing AI models with minimal disruption.

---

### Open Standards

Prefer technologies that support:

- Open APIs
- Standard protocols
- Portable data formats
- Interoperability

---

### Documentation-Driven Knowledge

Important engineering knowledge should be documented rather than relying on individual contributors or AI conversations.

Documentation should remain the authoritative source of architectural decisions.

---

### Periodic Technology Reviews

Major technology decisions should be reviewed regularly.

Technology adoption should be based on measurable engineering value rather than industry trends alone.

---

## Migration Strategy

When replacing a major technology:

1. Identify the need for change.
2. Evaluate candidate alternatives.
3. Apply the Builder Evaluation Matrix.
4. Document the decision in a new ADR.
5. Update the Tooling Guide.
6. Update the Engineering Handbook.
7. Communicate the change to project contributors.

---

## Technology Evaluation Criteria

Future technologies should be evaluated using factors such as:

- Maintainability
- Community adoption
- Documentation quality
- Ecosystem maturity
- Performance
- Security
- Cost
- Learning curve
- Long-term sustainability

---

## Rationale

A replaceable architecture:

- Reduces vendor lock-in
- Extends project longevity
- Encourages objective technology evaluation
- Simplifies future migrations
- Supports sustainable engineering practices

---

## Alternatives Considered

### Vendor-Centric Architecture

Advantages:

- Optimized for a specific platform

Disadvantages:

- Increased migration costs
- Vendor dependency
- Reduced flexibility

---

### Frequent Technology Replacement

Advantages:

- Access to the newest tools

Disadvantages:

- Constant migration effort
- Reduced engineering stability
- Increased maintenance costs

---

## Consequences

### Positive

- Long-term maintainability
- Technology flexibility
- Lower migration risk
- Sustainable engineering governance

### Trade-offs

- Requires periodic review effort
- May delay adoption of emerging technologies until adequately evaluated
