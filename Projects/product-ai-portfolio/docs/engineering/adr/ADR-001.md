# ADR-001: AI-Lab Multi-Agent Development Workflow

## Purpose

Define the standard AI-assisted development workflow for all AI-Lab projects by assigning clear responsibilities to each platform based on its strengths.

---

## Context

AI-Lab is intended to host multiple long-lived software products and experiments spanning product management, AI engineering, web applications, and research initiatives.

No single AI platform currently provides best-in-class capabilities across every stage of the software development lifecycle. Attempting to standardize on a single AI assistant introduces unnecessary compromises and increases the risk of vendor lock-in.

The objective is therefore to define a modular, role-based workflow where each tool is selected according to its strengths while remaining replaceable in the future.

---

## Decision

AI-Lab adopts a **Multi-Agent Development Workflow**.

Each platform is assigned a clearly defined responsibility rather than acting as a universal assistant.

## Tool Selection Principles

### Best Tool for the Job

Each tool should be used only for the responsibilities where it provides the greatest value.

### Modular Architecture

Every platform should be replaceable with minimal impact on the overall workflow.

### Avoid Vendor Lock-in

The workflow should remain portable across models, IDEs, hosting providers, and AI assistants.

### Documentation Before Implementation

Architecture and product decisions should be documented before coding begins.

### Human-in-the-Loop

The Product Owner retains responsibility for approving major architectural, product, and design decisions.

AI assists decision-making but does not replace it.

### Cost-Conscious Engineering

Free or low-cost tools should be preferred unless a paid solution delivers a demonstrable productivity improvement.

## Rationale

The selected workflow aligns with AI-Lab principles:

- Architecture-first development
- Documentation-first governance
- Separation of product and engineering concerns
- Sustainable tooling
- Flexibility to evolve with the AI ecosystem

---

## Alternatives Considered

A single-tool workflow was considered but rejected due to vendor lock-in, capability gaps, and reduced flexibility.

---

## Consequences

### Positive

- Clear responsibilities for every platform
- Reduced vendor dependency
- Easier onboarding
- Consistent documentation standards
- Scalable engineering methodology

### Trade-offs

- Team members must become familiar with multiple tools
- Workflow orchestration requires discipline
- Context occasionally needs to be transferred between tools
